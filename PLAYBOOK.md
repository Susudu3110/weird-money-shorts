# Weird Money — Channel Playbook

Every script gets scored against this BEFORE it's animated. The idea gets the
hours, not the motion. (Lessons from a 25-video hit-vs-flop study of "what if"
channels — same-channel comparisons, so style cancels out. It's a pattern, not
a law — n is small and retention data was unavailable — but it's testable.)

## The 6-point checklist (score each video idea before production)

1. **Premise = something the viewer wants TODAY, not a way to fix the past.**
   Money stories: found / lost / scammed / given-by-mistake money the viewer can
   picture themselves touching. "Restart life / undo mistakes" premises flopped
   0-for-3 in the study. Never write one.
2. **The title has a concrete noun people compare themselves on + stakes.**
   Wealth, salary, debt, time. Money is the strongest comparison noun there is.
   Put the dollar figure IN the title: "The $18,000,000..." beats "The maple
   syrup heist..." (An idea lost 1.3M → 57k views when the title's noun changed
   from wealth to partner count. Same title shape, same channel.)
3. **Open on the thing already working. Do not announce the title.**
   Only 1 of 15 hits asked its title question in the first 5 seconds. Open on
   the money already moving (the heist in progress, the wrong number on the
   check). Ask the framing question later — or never.
4. **Editing is not where views come from.**
   Cuts per minute: winners ≈ losers (≈11/min). The 5M-view winner had the
   FEWEST cuts. Never spend hours polishing motion instead of the idea.
5. **Don't judge an idea on a new channel's numbers.**
   The same idea on two channels: 357k vs <2k views. If video #1 gets 300
   views, the idea isn't disproven — the channel just has no audience yet.
   Judge premises on their 5th+ outing.
6. **End by handing the question back to the viewer.** (Comment bait =
   engagement signal, and comments are free distribution.)

## Quarterly (not per video): run the study in OUR niche

Ask Claude: "Pick ~20 true-money-story channels. From each, take their best and
worst shorts. Compare each channel with itself (same style/voice/audience) and
list what the winners share and what the losers share — titles, first 5
seconds, premises, endings." Grows more accurate with each channel added.
